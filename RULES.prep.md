# Enjeu: Preparation

**An expansion module.** The base game is complete without it. Leave these cards
in the box and nothing else changes.

Needs [Terrain](RULES.terrain.md) in play, because the wind-up is one of its
bricks and the props hand out its Marks.

**What it adds:** a way to spend this turn on the next one.

---

## 1. Prepare

Every action in the base game happens the instant you take it. There is no way
to say "I am setting something up", and setting something up is most of what
makes a fight feel like a plan.

> **Prepare.** One action, and it must be the **last action of your turn**.
> No bet, no check. Put the **yellow brick** under your figure.
>
> You are **Charged**: your next landed attack deals **+50**. You lose it the
> moment a hit costs you a card.

### Why it has to be last

This is the whole module, so it is worth one paragraph.

A Strike is one action for 25. If you could Prepare and then swing on the same
turn, Prepare would just be a Strike that deals 50, and the safest player at the
table would take it every single round for free. We measured that version: a
player who never bets a life card went from winning 47.6% of level 1 to winning
**100%** of it.

Made the last thing you do, it is a bet instead. The Charge has to live through
the boss's turn, and the boss's turn is exactly when you get hit, and getting
hit is what takes it away. Same card, opposite meaning.

### What takes it away

Any hit that costs you a card. Guarding with a Ready card, breaking a Spent one,
or a card breaking under Rage: all of them are the fight reaching you, and all of
them cost you the wind-up.

There is no exception for who made you pay. A Mark ticking counts. Being
poisoned while trying to wind up is meant to be hard.

## 2. Props

A **prop** is one big thing in the room. Draw one at the start of a level,
**build it out of bricks**, and stand it on the table where everyone can see it.

> **One action, one use.** Using it means knocking your own build over, so the
> build is the memory and there is nothing to write down.

| Prop | Element | Actions | What knocking it over does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brazier** | Fire | 1 | 25, and it is Burning |
| **Pitch Barrel** | Fire | 1 | 50, and it is Burning |
| **Fishing Net** | Water | 1 | 25, and it is Snared |
| **Cart** | none | 1 | 50, and it is Snared |
| **Signal Bell** | Wind | 1 | 25, and it is Marked |
| **Rope Bridge** | Wind | 1 | No damage. It is Frozen and loses its next turn |
| **Millstone** | Earth | 2 | 100 |
| **Chandelier** | none | 1 | 100, and **you** are Frozen: you lose your next action |

### One object a level, not two

If the place you are fighting in also handed you something (a Lava Vent, a
Rockfall), then **both are on the table and you may use one of them**, not both.

A level has one object and one use, whichever modules are in play. That rule is
what stops this module quietly doubling the free damage in every fight, and it
means a table running Terrain alone plays exactly as it did before.

### The two that cost you something

The **Chandelier** and the **Millstone** are the ones to look at twice. One
costs your next action, the other costs two actions up front. They are the
biggest numbers in the module and they are bets, like everything else here.

The **Rope Bridge** deals nothing at all and is often the strongest card in the
deck, because a boss that loses a turn is a boss that deals no damage for a
round. Cut it before a Rage round, not after.

---

## 3. The two halves are the same sentence

Prepare and the props are the same idea pointed twice: **pay now, collect later,
lose it if the fight gets to you first.**

The Rope Bridge is the pairing worth finding on your own. Freeze the boss, and
the Charge you took last turn lives through a turn where nothing could take it.

## Quick reference

- Prepare is the last action of your turn. Yellow brick, +50 on the next landed hit.
- Any hit that costs you a card costs you the Charge.
- One prop a level. Build it, knock it over once.
- A level has one object and one use, even with both modules in play.
- The Chandelier and the Millstone cost you something. That is the point.
