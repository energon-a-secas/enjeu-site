# Enjeu: Wits

**An expansion module.** The base game is complete without it. Leave these cards
in the box and nothing else changes.

Needs [Terrain](RULES.terrain.md) in play, because two of the three cards put one
of its bricks under a figure.

**What it adds:** three cards for the turns you would rather find something out
than hit something.

---

## 1. Three cards, not three buttons

They go in your hand, next to Strike and Focus, and you play them exactly the way
you play those: pick the card, spend the action, roll the check.

That is deliberate. This is the module for the player who does not reach for the
attack cards, and an option you have to remember is not an option. These are
lying in front of you with the rest.

| Card | Actions | Bet | Check | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Scout** | 1 | none | Even | Throw the boss's die now, face up. That is the reaction it will make this round. |
| **Analyze** | 1 | none | Even | The boss or one minion is **Marked**: attacks against it are one step easier. |
| **Parley** | 1 | none | Hard | Once a level. It loses its action this round. Fail, and it **Roars** at you instead. |

None of them deals any damage. None of them bets a life card.

---

## 2. Scout is the question, Parley is the answer

Parley is a Hard check, which you will fail three times in four, and failing does
not simply waste the action: the boss's reaction this round becomes a **Roar**,
whatever it was going to be.

So the interesting thing is not whether Parley works. It is **what you are
trading away**.

- It was going to **Ruin** you for double damage, and you turned that into a
  Roar. Even failing was a good round.
- It was going to **Brace**, which costs you nothing at all, and you turned that
  into a Roar that hurts. That is the worst move in the module.

The only way to know which one you are buying is to have looked first. That is
what Scout is for, and it is why two of your three actions is the honest price of
a plan.

**Play them in that order and the module has a shape.** Play Parley blind and it
is a coin you flip at yourself.

---

## 3. The Scout Board

Your table has one die. Scout throws it as the boss's die, and then you need it
back for your own checks, so the number has to live somewhere.

The board has six rows, one per face. **Stand a brick on the row the die
landed on**, and take the die back. When the boss acts this round, it makes the
reaction on that row.

Any brick will do. It is the same trick the Marks use: the object on the table
is the memory, so nobody has to hold a number in their head.

---

## 4. Why none of this helps a coward

Worth saying plainly, because it is the test this module had to pass.

Marked makes a check one step easier. **Strike has no check**, so Marked does
nothing for it. A player who never bets a life card plays nothing but Strike, so
Analyze is worth exactly zero to them. Scout sells you the boss's number, which
is only useful if you were deciding how much to hold back, and a player holding
everything back has already decided. Parley is barred from the Rage round on,
which is when a cautious player is finally in trouble.

The module is for the player who is already spending. It cannot rescue one who
is not, and that is on purpose: an expansion that fixes the base game stops being
optional.

## Quick reference

- Three cards in your hand: look, study, talk.
- Scout throws the boss's die face up. Stand a brick on the board.
- Analyze Marks a figure: one step easier to hit, until a hit lands.
- Parley is once a level, Hard, and failing turns the boss's move into a Roar.
- Look before you talk.
